# Craft: how a Hububb post is written

Distilled from three sources. Read them when a rule needs its evidence:
- `research/meta-ads-library/synthesis.md` and per-advertiser files: what long-running paid ads from Blueground, Hostfully, OpenRent, Hospitable, Guesty, Lodgify, Hostaway have in common (Oct 2026).
- `research/paper-references/analysis.md`: six rounds of Andrej and Peter's comments on 157 Hububb Host and Stay drafts (Oct 2026). This is the team's own taste, so it outranks the competitor research when they disagree.
- `docs/strategy-context.md`: pillars, funnel, voice, caption policy.

The golden library (`social/golden/`) outranks all three.

## 1. Owner framing (the Blueground lesson Andrej chose)

Host posts talk to people who own or run a place, about what the place earns and what it asks of them. Not about software.

- **Name the reader by situation, not by "host".** One flat and a day job. Three flats and too many changeovers. A London place that hits the 90-night cap in spring. A management company with owners to answer to. Use the persona's facts.
- **Frame additively.** Keep, add, on top, fill, without leaving. The listing stays on Airbnb; longer stays come in on top; short stays fill the gaps. Never ask the reader to give something up or switch everything.
- **Outcome in calendar, time, or money terms, never feature terms.** "The calendar fills in blocks" beats "multi-calendar sync". "One check-in, then a month of quiet" beats "guest management".
- **The calendar is the hero object.** Longer stays anchor the year, short stays fill the gaps. When a post needs one image of the product's value, it is a calendar filling in blocks, not a dashboard.
- **Hostie is how it runs without you, not the headline act.** Lead with the outcome (longer stays, quiet evenings), let Hostie be the mechanism in the subhead or a slide.

## 2. Headline mechanics

Write at least six candidates per post from different rows, then keep the best. Examples are Hububb-shaped and truthful; competitor lines are evidence, never copy.

| Mechanic | How it works | Evidence | Hububb-shaped example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Already-done moment | Line 1 the event, line 2 it is handled. Past tense, no verb of effort | Team praised "Guest checked out / Clean already booked" | 11.47pm question / Already answered |
| Time-stamped scene | A clock time or day plus one real detail | Hostfully's "It's 2am", team's "Sunday night is / yours again" (loved) | Sunday night is / yours again |
| Contrast couplet | Two lines, same rhythm, opposite meaning | "Too long for a hotel / Too short for a lease" (Paper, Stay) | Longer stays anchor the year / Short stays fill the gaps |
| One hero number | One number carries the whole post and recurs in the caption | OpenRent's "6 days" ran 19 months; Hububb's 90 / 275 (now on legal hold, see section 8) | 35 nights / One check-in |
| You / we split | The reader keeps something, we take something | Paper "You provide / the space", repo "You keep the listing / We take the admin" | You keep the listing / We bring the longer stays |
| Named reader question | Role or situation in the first five words, as a question | Blueground "Running 5+ short-term rentals?", OpenRent "Landlord with an empty property?" | One flat and a day job? |
| Additive "without" | The outcome plus the fear removed | Blueground "without leaving your short-term platforms" | Monthly guests / without leaving Airbnb |
| Micro-chore | Name the smallest real chore, not the category | Hospitable "hot tub prep", OpenRent crossed-out to-do list | The wifi password / at 11pm / handled |
| Identity by exclusion | Say what it is not | Hostfully "Not enterprise. Not landlords. You.", repo "Not a takeover. Not a PMS" | Not a takeover / Not a PMS |
| Reframe the easy part | The obvious step is easy, the hard part follows | Hospitable "Listing your place is the easy part" | Getting booked was / the easy part |
| Pain triplet, short dismissal | Three concrete pains, then two or three words | Hostfully "Double bookings, 3am pings, and OTA fees? Not anymore" (30+ ads) | Midweek gaps / Sunday changeovers / 11pm questions / Handled |
| Brand sign-off | A closing line that could only be ours, for last slides | Peter liked "Make room for longer stays" as a close | Make room for / longer stays |

## 3. Line rules (on-image)

- **Headline:** 2 to 7 words (team median 5), two lines max. Line 2 is the payoff; the designer may set it grey or about 80 percent.
- **No full stops at the end of on-image lines.** The team calls them "a giveaway that it's AI generated". A two-beat headline turns on a line break, not a period. Question marks are fine.
- **Subhead:** 6 to 14 words, mechanism or proof only. Never a second hook. Same size as the headline is fine (the team liked it).
- **Chips, labels, card text:** 2 to 5 words.
- **No layer repeats another layer's phrasing.** Across headline, subhead, caption first line, and CTA, no run of three or more words appears twice. Key nouns and the hero number may recur (that is how a post holds together); the caption first line says the hook again in new words. For a carousel the layers are slide 1, the caption first line, and the CTA; for a reel, the first super, the caption first line, and the CTA.
- **Counting words:** a time stamp ("11.47pm") counts as one word; text inside a phone or a UI card does not count toward a beat or headline limit.
- **Times** are written UK style: 11.47pm, 9am, Sunday 9pm.
- **One idea per frame.** If a slide needs "and", it is two slides.
- Sentence case, capital first letter. No exclamation marks. No em or en dashes. No emoji on image.

## 4. Formats

### Static (4:5 default, 1:1 when asked)
Headline, optional subhead, optional CTA pill. Lifestyle image does most of the work; type sits in its negative space. One proof device at most (a done card, a calendar block, a chip).

### Carousel (3 to 6 slides, 4:5)
- Slide 1 is the most visual and carries only the hook. It must work as a single post in the grid.
- Middle slides: one mechanism or proof each, each with its own proof device.
- Last slide resolves: a brand line (sign-off mechanic) and, on convert posts, the CTA pill.
- Up to 12 words per slide. Swipe cue only when the hook does not already pull.
- Shapes that the team has reviewed (pick one, name it in the plan): problem, mechanism, resolve (3); split promise (3); you, we, brand (3); tension to resolve (4); how it works journey (5); explainer opening "What is Hububb?" (6, Kindred and Casa style, must not be text heavy); one slide per audience segment (corporate, relocation, between homes).

### Reel (7 to 15s brand, up to 30s demo, 9:16 with a 4:5 safe zone)
- A timed beat sheet: one idea per beat, 1.5 to 4s per beat, five or fewer words on screen per beat.
- Frame one carries the hook as burned-in text: a scene, a POV line, or the named-reader question.
- Pace fast; no line holds longer than it takes to read. Type moves on opacity and travel only, never scaled or stretched. Weights 400 to 600.
- Outro is the logo only. No slogan, handle, and CTA pile-up.
- Captions on if there is any voice. Default is no voiceover.

### Story (9:16)
- All type in the top 40 percent, bottom 20 percent clear for stickers and the reply bar.
- The headline must carry the message alone; nobody reads a story caption.
- Usually a cut of a feed post. Owner-facing stories never use urgency.

## 5. Caption pack

- **First line:** the only line before "more", under 125 characters, stands alone, re-voices the slide-1 or static headline in caption form. Per account: host the emotional hook; company the thesis plainly; work the concrete benefit; stay the lifestyle image.
- **Body:** 70 to 100 words (the team's captions ran 74 to 98), sentences of 7 to 12 words, normal punctuation. Shape: the moment, what usually happens, what happens with Hububb, one allowed proof claim if it fits, the CTA.
- **Endings:** host ends on a single action plus "Link in bio." (CTA verb: "List on Hububb" or "Start with one listing"); company ends on the thesis or a follow reason, no hard CTA; stay ends on a soft tagline ("Stay a month, not a night"), no hard CTA while dormant; work ends on a plain next step only once the conversion action is confirmed, otherwise the benefit.
- **Hashtags:** host 3 to 5, company 2 to 4, work 3 to 5, stay 3 to 5. Specific and audience-real (#londonhosts, #midtermrental, #serviceprovidersuk), never reach tags or walls.
- No emoji in captions.

## 6. Paid cut (only when asked)

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Primary text | 15 to 30 words. Line one names the reader and promises the outcome; it must work before the "more" cut (about 125 characters). |
| Headline (link headline) | 3 to 6 words, verb-led, ends with a full stop: "Fill the nights after 90." It sits under the creative, so a period is fine here. |
| Description | Optional, under 8 words, the objection answer: "Your listing stays on Airbnb." |
| CTA button | From Meta's set. Learn More when the reader is trusting us with a home; Sign Up when it is listing a unit. Stay: Learn More only while dormant. |

Keep the four layers (primary text, on-image headline, subhead, link headline) free of repeated words. When a message wins, keep the words and rotate the visual: suggest two alternate images per paid cut.

## 7. CTA vocabulary

- **Host:** "List on Hububb" (the team's latest), "List your unit", "Start with one listing". Never "Connect your Airbnb" as the on-image CTA. Black pill on light, white pill on dark.
- **Company:** no on-image CTA.
- **Stay:** none on image while dormant; soft tagline in caption.
- **Work:** none until the conversion action is confirmed.

## 8. Truth guardrails

**Precedence.** When sources disagree about what the product does, this section wins, then `products/<slug>/product-marketing.md`, then the persona files. Persona files supply the situation (who, where, what hurts, their words); they are not product truth. Ignore any product promise, price, tier, or "drafted, you review" framing inside a persona's prompts or value promise that this section does not support.

Global: only the product's `allowedProofClaims`, verbatim and in their stated context. No figures from the website (Omar K., listing prices, ratings) unless they are added to `qa.json`. No competitor names. No sibling capabilities. No unbuilt features. No named-host faces.

**Sibling-list traps.** QA matches sibling phrases literally. On Host, "stay a month" and "book your stay" are Stay's: write "guests who book a month or more". On Stay, "Hostie" and "dynamic pricing" are Host's. On Work, "stay a month" is Stay's, except inside Stay's own tagline on a Stay post.

**Host (positioning updated 14 Oct 2026: longer stays plus Hostie)**
- Longer stays are 28 nights or more, from corporate and relocation guests (and people between homes), promoted through Hububb Stay. Short stays fill the gaps on the host's other channels (Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo, Expedia), priced automatically.
- The listing stays where it is; reviews and Superhost status stay with the host; disconnect any time; connect one unit first.
- Hostie answers guests automatically, in the host's voice. Never "drafted for you to review", never "you approve". The qa.json chip "AI-drafted replies. You review and send." is superseded; do not use it.
- Turnover cleans are booked automatically at checkout. Vetted cleaners, handymen, locksmiths, electricians are booked from the app and billed at cost.
- **90 nights, LEGAL HOLD (14 Oct 2026).** You may say London caps entire-home short lets at 90 nights a year. Do not say or imply that monthly stays sit outside the cap, that Hububb "fills the other 275", or that longer stays get around the rule: the 1973 Act defines short-term use as the same guest for fewer than 90 consecutive nights, so a 28 to 89 night stay may still count. The live site's FAQ and the golden 90/275 line both make that claim; both are on hold until legal confirms. If the user asks for a 90-night post anyway, write it, and put the hold in the post's truth flag.
- No occupancy guarantees: never "guaranteed", "100%", "never empty", "always booked".
- No prices. `qa.json` says £35, the live site says £43; leave price out until Andrej confirms.
- **Usable Host proof today** (verbatim, in context): "tested on hundreds of real London properties"; "processed millions in revenue" (pricing only); "running hundreds of cleans monthly" (cleaning only); "Handles 90%+ of the day-to-day."; "Set up in under fifteen minutes."; "A built-in network of vetted local cleaners."; "One plan, everything included." Not usable: the four £35 claims (price open) and "AI-drafted replies. You review and send." (superseded).
- HomeHold (a guest in the property adds another month in one tap) may be described; never with a percentage.
- Not a takeover, not the legal property manager. We take the admin: guest messages, cleans, pricing.

**Stay**
- Book by the month at furnished, managed homes; cleaning and upkeep taken care of. Adding another month is real (HomeHold).
- Not live, never state as fact: butler or concierge, FLEX, PASS, TRIPS, personalised stays, corporate booking as a product.
- Unconfirmed, do not state until approved: no deposit, 24/7 human support, keyless entry, "within minutes".
- Soft, no hard CTA while dormant. No proof claims exist yet.

**Work**
- Jobs come from Hububb properties; the job comes with access, scope, and standard up front; payment runs through the app. Trades: cleaners, handymen, locksmiths, pest control.
- No pay rates, earnings, or job counts. The consumer name (Work vs the site's "Hububb Connect") is unresolved; say "Hububb Work" or just "Hububb".
- How far "steady" goes: turnover work recurs by nature (every checkout needs a clean), and that may be said. Never promise volume, a full week, guaranteed hours, or "recurring bookings" as a commitment.

**Company (@wearehububb)**
- The one account that names Host, Stay, and Work together. Tagline: "For the feeling of home, everywhere you go."
- Proof only from `products/general/qa.json`. "Innovative" and "solution" are banned by QA.
- Tease the vision lightly; FLEX, PASS, TRIPS, personalised stays, corporate booking stay off.

## 9. Anti-patterns

From the Meta research and the team's rejections. Any of these in a draft means rewrite:
- Feature inventories or checklists ("Channel manager, unified inbox, owner reports..."). Ticks are fine as a proof device on a slide, not as a copy strategy.
- Title Case slogans, hype verbs, "#1", "the future of", urgency on an owner decision.
- Questions as lazy openers ("Are you tired of", "Did you know", "Looking to").
- A second hook in the subhead. A headline that needs the caption to make sense.
- Copy the visual cannot prove ("We handle everything" over a generic photo). The team asked, round after round, for the visual to demonstrate the line.
- Generic "host" with no situation, no time, no number.
- Periods on image. Logos and product labels stacked on the frame ("Hububb Host" footers were removed every round).
- Reprinting a golden or past line verbatim in a new plan without being asked. Past lines live in `social/plans/`, `creatives/*/brief.json` (`copy` and `social` fields), and the Paper drafts in `research/paper-references/analysis.md` sections 2 to 5.
