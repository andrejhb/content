## Analysis

### A. Core angles / pillars
1. **Layer, don't switch** (operators): keep your STR listings, add monthly guests on top. "Keep your listings live and layer monthly guests on top."
2. **Demand you can't reach alone** (operators): 4,000+ companies, 50+ countries, 100,000+ homes. "Bookings from corporates you've never pitched."
3. **Less work per booking** (operators): "One check-in a month. Not thirty." "fewer cleans, less messaging, steadier income."
4. **Low friction to join**: "Connect your PMS. Done." "Plug in. Start booking." Older: "List for free", "Just 7% commission".
5. **Guaranteed, hands-off income** (individual owners): "Guaranteed monthly rent, even between tenants." "Fill in the form, sign, collect the rent."
6. **Better than a hotel / than Airbnb for 30+ days** (HR, guests): "More room than a hotel." "Just can't go back to Airbnb." "no nightly rate math and no surprise fees."

### Hook patterns (first line of primary text)
- Qualifier question that names the reader by portfolio size: "Running 5+ short-term rentals?", "Manage 5+ furnished rentals?", "Already on a PMS?", "Own 5+ Furnished Properties?"
- Role call-out with a colon: "HR leaders:", "HR and mobility teams:"
- Reframed pain as a statement: "Slow months don't have to mean empty units." "30+ days in a short-term rental costs more than you think."
- Geo question for owners: "Have a property in Athens?" "Do you own a property in Luxembourg City?"
- Retargeting nudge: "Still thinking about that apartment?"

### Headline formulas
- On-image headline, 3 to 7 words, two lines, sentence case, ends with a full stop only when it is a two-beat line: "Connect your PMS. Done." / "One check-in a month. Not thirty." / "Same standard. Every city and stay."
- Possessive outcome: "Your low season, booked".
- Category claim: "The established network for monthly-plus stays", "A global network you can trust".
- Link headline under the creative is always a verb-led restatement, 3 to 6 words, full stop: "Mix in monthly bookings." "Plug in. Start booking." "Trade turnovers for monthly stays." "Keep your calendar full year-round."

### Primary text length and structure
- Operator ads: 1 to 2 sentences, 15 to 20 words. No emoji, no bullets, no line breaks. Question hook + one-sentence answer.
- Guest ads: 4 to 6 lines. Hook sentence + benefit sentence + 3 checkmark bullets (✔) + city CTA line. Retargeting uses emoji bullets (⏳ 🧳 📅).
- Older operator ads (Apr to Oct 2026 winner) used checkmark bullets with a hard number: "✓ Just 7% commission".

### CTA labels
- Operators: Sign Up (current), Learn More (older). On-image pill duplicates the CTA ("Sign Up ->").
- Owners: Learn More, on-image "See how it works", "Start now".
- Guests: Check Availability (prospecting), Book Now (DPA, retargeting).
- HR: Learn More.

### Proof devices
- Scale numbers in the subhead: "50+ countries · 100,000+ homes", "4,000+ companies", "8,000+ owners".
- Guarantees: "100% occupancy, every month", "Guaranteed monthly rent, even between tenants."
- Price transparency: "Just 7% commission", "No upfront cost", "$2,850 per month".
- Inventory counts per city in link descriptions: "1997 fully furnished apartments in NYC".
- Social proof: review card "Just can't go back to Airbnb." with named reviewer and job title.

### Offer framing
- Operators: free to join, commission only, no switching cost ("Keep the system you already use").
- Owners: guaranteed rent, full management, 3-step onboarding.
- Guests: no nightly math, no surprise fees, Wi-Fi and utilities included, seasonal promo codes up to $100 off.

### Visual direction
- Owner and operator: one consistent system. Sky-blue gradient, BLUEGROUND wordmark top left, 2-line bold headline in white or navy, one line of small subhead, navy pill button, then a polished low-angle portrait of a confident, smiling adult leaning on a building. Lots of sky. The person is the owner archetype, not a guest. No UI, no app screens, no icons.
- HR: same frame, but conceptual miniature rooms held in hands.
- Guests: real interiors, review-card mockups, and phone-native POV video with caption text for promo bursts.

## B. Deep dive: how the owner/operator copy is built

1. **Three layers, each with one job.**
   - Primary text = qualify + promise. "Running 5+ short-term rentals?" filters out hobby hosts before the image is even read.
   - On-image headline = the outcome, 3 to 7 words. "Your low season, booked".
   - On-image subhead = proof + objection handling in one line. "Monthly-plus stays from 4,000+ companies -- on top of your short-term calendar."
   - Link headline = the action restated as a verb phrase. "Keep your calendar full year-round."
   The three never repeat each other word for word; each layer adds a new reason.
2. **Headline length.** 4 to 7 words, broken into two short lines, sentence case. Two-beat versions use a full stop as the turn: "Connect your PMS. Done." The second beat is shorter than the first, which gives a confident landing.
3. **Subhead pattern A, the stat line.** Number · number, then a dash and a plain-English payoff: "50+ countries · 100,000+ homes -- monthly-plus demand, one connection." The middot separates proof units so they read like a spec sheet, not a boast. For Hububb, swap the dash for a colon or a full stop.
4. **Subhead pattern B, "without X" / "on top of X".** The objection an operator would raise (I would have to leave Airbnb, I would lose my contracts, I would need new software) is pre-answered inside the subhead: "Vetted, longer-stay demand -- without leaving your short-term platforms." "on top of your existing contracts." "Keep the system you already use." Additive framing ("add", "layer", "on top", "mix in") is the spine of the whole campaign. Nothing asks the reader to give anything up.
5. **Subhead pattern C, the three-item consequence list.** "fewer cleans, less messaging, steadier income." Three short noun phrases, comparative adjectives, ends on money.
6. **Audience framing.** The reader is a professional operator, addressed by portfolio size and tooling ("5+", "PMS", "account list", "sales team"). Owners are addressed by city ("your Athens property"). HR is addressed by job title. Never "hosts" in general.
7. **Tone.** Calm, adult, factual. No exclamation marks in the current set (older ads had them and were replaced). No emoji in operator copy. No hype words (no "revolutionary", "game-changing"). Verbs are mechanical and simple: plug in, add, layer, keep, swap, fill.
8. **CTA.** One CTA, mirrored on-image as a pill. "Sign Up" for operators (low commitment, software-like), "Learn More" for owners handing over a home (higher trust needed).

What makes it feel premium and trustworthy:
- One visual system repeated across every variant, so the brand becomes familiar fast.
- Hard numbers with a plus sign (4,000+, 100,000+, 50+), never vague "thousands".
- Generous negative space, one idea per frame, short lines.
- Real-looking, well-lit people photographed from below: confidence and ownership, not stock-office smiles.
- Additive, low-risk framing removes the fear of switching.
- Restraint: no stickers, no badges, no starbursts, no urgency on the owner side.
