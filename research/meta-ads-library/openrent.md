## Analysis

### Core angles / pillars
1. **Speed to let, one number.** "6 days" in every headline. The number is the brand.
2. **Saving vs the high-street agent.** "£1,849 Saved per property (average)", "saved over a £1200 in fees compared to my usual letting agent", "HIGH STREET AGENTS HATE US".
3. **Scale and default choice.** "More landlords use us than any other letting agent in the UK", "7 million+", "UK Landlords' no.1 agent".
4. **Peer proof.** Trustpilot "EXCELLENT", a named landlord quote with real numbers ("31 enquiries").
5. **Chore removal.** The crossed-out to-do list: everything a landlord has to organise, replaced by one action.

### Hook patterns
- "Landlord + situation?" question: "Landlord worried about an empty property?", "Landlord with an empty property?", "Landlord looking for new tenants?". The reader's role is the first word.
- Imperative + list of portals: "Advertise your rental property: Rightmove, Zoopla, OpenRent and more."
- Category claim: "More landlords use us than any other letting agent in the UK."

### Headline formulas
- Outcome + number: "Find new tenants in 6 days".
- Love statement: "Landlords love OpenRent" (even "Landlords❤️OpenRent").
- Rank claim: "UK Landlords' no.1 agent".

### Length and structure
- 2 to 3 short sentences, 20 to 30 words, ends with a soft, friendly CTA question "Why not try us out?" / "Why not join in?".
- Stat sheet: one number per line, no emoji, no bullets, plain label "(average)" for honesty.
- No emoji in primary text except a heart in one headline.

### CTA labels
- Learn More, See details. Low pressure.

### Proof devices
- Averages stated as averages: "6 days until let (average)", "£1,849 Saved per property (average)". The qualifier makes the number credible.
- Trustpilot rating word ("EXCELLENT") rather than a score.
- Named customer quote with a precise count.

### Offer framing
- "Get Started for free". Price comparison is against agents' fees, not competitors' software.

### Visual direction
- Deliberately unpolished and human: handwriting on lined paper, a text-message screenshot, a real-looking older landlord vacuuming, a meme-ish angry-agent photo. Simple flat blue panels with condensed caps type. Feels like a landlord made it, which matches a self-managing audience.
