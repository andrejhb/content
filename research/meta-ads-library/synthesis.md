## B. Blueground deep dive (owner and operator copy)

Blueground runs one system across every owner/operator ad. Current examples (Oct 2026):

| Primary text (hook) | On-image headline | On-image subhead | Link headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running 5+ short-term rentals? Keep your listings live and layer monthly guests on top. | Add monthly stays to your mix | Vetted, longer-stay demand, without leaving your short-term platforms. | Mix in monthly bookings. |
| Manage 5+ furnished rentals? Plug into a network spanning 50+ countries with one connection. | A global network you can trust | 50+ countries · 100,000+ homes: monthly-plus demand, one connection. | Tap into global demand. |
| Already on a PMS? You're most of the way there. Plug in and start taking corporate bookings. | Connect your PMS. Done. | Keep the system you already use. Add corporate demand, cut guest turnover, and skip the extra staffing. | Plug in. Start booking. |
| Running 5+ short-term rentals? Swap constant turnovers for monthly guests. | One check-in a month. Not thirty. | Monthly guests: fewer cleans, less messaging, steadier income. | Trade turnovers for monthly stays. |
| Slow months don't have to mean empty units. Fill them with monthly corporate stays. | Your low season, booked | Monthly-plus stays from 4,000+ companies, on top of your short-term calendar. | Keep your calendar full year-round. |
| (Athens owners, GR) We guarantee 100% occupancy ... | Reliable tenants, stable income | Fill in the form, sign, collect the rent. | Reliable tenants / 100% occupancy, every month |

(Blueground's own subheads use an em dash where the colon or comma sits above.)

How it is built:
1. **Four layers, four jobs, no repetition.** Primary text qualifies and promises. On-image headline states the outcome. Subhead carries proof plus the objection answer. Link headline restates the action as a verb phrase with a full stop.
2. **Headline length**: 4 to 7 words on two lines. Sentence case. Two-beat versions turn on a full stop and the second beat is shorter ("Connect your PMS. Done.").
3. **Subhead pattern A, stat line**: "number · number" then a plain payoff. The middot makes proof read like a spec, not a boast.
4. **Subhead pattern B, objection handling**: "without leaving your short-term platforms", "on top of your existing contracts", "Keep the system you already use." The whole campaign is additive: add, layer, mix in, on top, keep. Nothing asks the reader to give anything up.
5. **Subhead pattern C, three consequences ending on money**: "fewer cleans, less messaging, steadier income."
6. **Audience framing**: the reader is addressed by portfolio size ("5+"), tooling ("PMS"), or city ("your Athens property"), never as a generic "host".
7. **Tone**: calm, adult, factual. No exclamation marks (older ads had them and were retired). No emoji on owner/operator ads. Simple mechanical verbs: plug in, add, keep, swap, fill.
8. **CTA**: one CTA, mirrored as an on-image pill. Sign Up for operators (software-like, low commitment). Learn More for owners handing over a home.

Why it feels premium and trustworthy: one repeated visual system (sky gradient, wordmark top left, navy pill, real-looking person photographed from below against open sky), hard numbers with a plus sign, generous space, one idea per frame, no stickers or badges, no urgency on the owner side, and additive framing that removes switching risk. The historic winner "Own 5+ Furnished Properties?" (18 versions, 21 Apr to 7 Oct 2026) used the same skeleton with checkmark bullets and a hard price: "✓ Just 7% commission".

## C. Cross-advertiser patterns

### Reusable copy mechanics (with evidence)

1. **Qualifier hook: role + size or situation in the first five words.** "Running 5+ short-term rentals?" (Blueground), "Landlord with an empty property?" (OpenRent), "Multi-property owner/manager?" (Hostaway), "HR leaders:" (Blueground).
2. **One hero number, repeated everywhere.** OpenRent's "6 days" is in the hook, the headline and the image, and has run since March 2025. Blueground's "5+", Hostfully's "30,000+".
3. **Two-beat headline turning on a full stop.** "One check-in a month. Not thirty." / "It's 2 a.m. A real human answers." (Hostfully) / "Fewer tools. Less daily admin." (Hospitable) / "44% More Listings. Real Results." (Hostaway)
4. **Additive framing that kills switching fear.** "without leaving your short-term platforms" (Blueground), "Connect your go-to tools instead of replacing them." (Hospitable), "test it out before you turn off your old software" (Guesty).
5. **Spec-line proof with separators.** "50+ countries · 100,000+ homes" (Blueground), "4.9-star reviews. 90k users. 300+ integrations." (Hospitable).
6. **Honest averages.** "6 days until let (average)", "£1,849 Saved per property (average)" (OpenRent), "grew revenue an average +33% in their first year" (Guesty). The qualifier buys belief.
7. **Pain triplet, then a two-word dismissal.** "Double bookings, 3am pings, and OTA fees? 👋 Not anymore." (Hostfully, its single biggest message, 30+ ads).
8. **Time-stamped scene.** "It's 2am 🌙 Sync breaks. A guest is standing at the wrong door." (Hostfully). "Answer check-in questions without putting down your paddle." (Hospitable).
9. **Micro-chore specificity beats category claims.** "Keep hot tub prep on schedule without texting your cleaner every time." (Hospitable). OpenRent's crossed-out list: "get gas certificate / find electrician / organise insurance ... use OpenRent".
10. **Reframe the easy part.** "Listing your place is the easy part. Then come the guest messages, calendar updates, and pricing decisions." (Hospitable). "A great listing needs more than great photos."
11. **Identity by exclusion.** "Built for property managers. Not enterprise. Not landlords. You." (Hostfully).
12. **Native-format creative that looks like the reader's own phone.** iPhone Notes "Green flags:" list (Hospitable), a text-message thread "It was let in 4 days and I saved over a £1200 in fees" (OpenRent), a review card "Just can't go back to Airbnb." (Blueground), POV caption video (Blueground holiday codes).
13. **Verb-led link headline with a full stop.** "Mix in monthly bookings." / "Keep your calendar full year-round." (Blueground). It reads like an instruction, not a slogan.
14. **Stakes of one failure.** "a missed or delayed message can cost you a 5-star review" (Hostaway), "save the stay before it costs you reviews" (Guesty), "One bad clean, one bad review." (Lodgify).
15. **Guarantee for owners.** "Guaranteed monthly rent, even between tenants." / "Fill in the form, sign, collect the rent." (Blueground Athens).

### What the long-running ads have in common
- OpenRent (79 of 82 ads past 30 days, oldest 19 months), Blueground's historic owner ads (6 to 12 months), Hostfully's "Not anymore" (30+ ads, 6+ weeks), Hostaway's Google partnership (16 weeks).
- Short primary text: 15 to 35 words, one or two sentences.
- The reader is named in the first line.
- Exactly one concrete number (6 days, 5+, 7%, 30,000+).
- Low-commitment CTA (Learn More, See details, Sign Up, free demo).
- The message stays fixed while images and link headlines rotate. Winners are run as 5 to 30 duplicates with different visuals, not rewritten.
- Plain claims with a qualifier or a source ("(average)", "on Trustpilot", "as of June 2026").

### Anti-patterns (generic, feature-list, hype)
- "#1" claims with no source: "Get the #1 software for your vacation rental business" appears almost word for word at Hostfully and Guesty, plus "#1 Vacation Rental Software" on every Lodgify creator ad.
- Checklist filler: "✅ Grow revenue ✅ Maximize occupancy ✅ Receive more 5-star reviews And so much more…" (interchangeable between brands).
- Title Case feature slogans: "Boost Revenue with Our Powerful PMS", "Take Your Bookings to New Heights", "Streamline Tasks, Delight Your Guests".
- Feature inventories: "Channel Manager, Owner Reports, Unified Inbox, Central Calendar, and 120+ integrations, all in one PMS."
- Hype verbs and emoji: "exploded revenue", "revenue rocket", "Supercharge your bookings!", "game-changer", "The future is here.", "🤯".
- Coupon and hashtag walls in creator ads (Lodgify): reads as influencer content, not a brand.
- Sloppiness that kills trust: "From Airbnb to Verb", "earning potential?.", "Hoppper.com".
- Urgency on high-trust decisions: countdowns work for software discounts, but no owner-side ad in the set uses urgency.

## D. Copy rules distilled (for the Hububb copywriting skill)

### Global rules
1. Name the reader in the first five words, by role and situation: "London host with 3+ flats?", "Own a flat that sits empty between lets?".
2. One idea and one number per ad. If two numbers, separate them with a middot in a spec line.
3. Four layers, four jobs, no repeated words between them: primary text (qualify + promise), on-image headline (outcome), on-image subhead (proof or "without X"), link headline (verb-led action with a full stop).
4. Headline: 3 to 7 words, max 2 lines, first letter capitalised, sentence case otherwise. A two-beat headline may turn on a full stop.
5. Never use em or en dashes. Use a colon, comma, full stop or middot. Blueground's dash device becomes "Vetted demand: no need to leave Airbnb." style.
6. Frame additively: keep, add, on top, without leaving, while you sleep. Do not ask the reader to give something up.
7. Proof must be specific and qualified: "(average)", "on Trustpilot", "across [N] London stays" (use only real, verified figures). Never "#1", never "best".
8. Name micro-chores, not categories: "the 11pm Wi-Fi password message", "the Sunday changeover clean", not "guest communication".
9. Product truth for Host: Hostie answers guests automatically, in the host's voice. Say "answered", never "drafted for you to review".
10. Emoji: none on owner, premium or parent-brand ads. At most one, used as punctuation, on host-ops ads. No hashtags in paid primary text.
11. No exclamation marks, no hype verbs (supercharge, skyrocket, unlock, game-changer, revolutionise).
12. CTA: Learn More when the reader hands over a home or trust; Sign Up / Get started / Download when it is software or a free resource. Mirror it as one pill on the image.
13. When a message wins, keep the words and rotate the image. Plan 3 to 5 visuals per message from day one.

### Instagram feed statics (1:1, 4:5)
- Primary text: 15 to 30 words, one or two sentences, qualifier hook first. Fits above the "more" fold (about 125 characters) without needing it.
- On image: wordmark top left, headline up to 7 words on 2 lines, one subhead line up to 14 words carrying proof or the objection answer, one pill.
- Visual: real lifestyle photo with a person or place and open space for type (Blueground sky, Hospitable golden hour). Monochrome with mint only on a small status element.
- Link headline: verb-led, 3 to 6 words, full stop. "Let Hostie take the night shift."

### Carousels
- Card 1: the hook as a headline plus a swipe cue ("Swipe" pill, as Hospitable's "SWIPE RIGHT").
- Card 2: the stakes or fear, said gently ("Handing over your property is a big decision").
- Cards 3 to 4: the mechanism and proof, one per card (three steps, a comparison, verified credentials, a real message thread).
- Final card: the outcome plus the CTA pill.
- One sentence per card, up to 12 words on image. Same link headline on every card.
- Strong shapes from the set: decision walk-through (Hospitable owners), 3 steps "Fill in the form, sign, collect the rent." (Blueground), crossed-out to-do list ending in the product (OpenRent).

### Reels and video
- Frame one carries burned-in text: a scene ("It's 2am. A guest can't find the key safe."), a POV ("POV: you landed in London for a month and it already feels like home."), or the qualifier question.
- Length: 7 to 15 seconds for brand and owner ads (Blueground runs 7 to 8 seconds), up to 30 seconds for product demos. Captions always on, one line at a time.
- Primary text can run 40 to 70 words in short paragraphs, but the first line must stand alone before truncation.
- Formats that earn impressions: POV phone footage with captions, a calm talking head (customer or host), UI shown only as a single card or phone, never a full desktop.
- Outro: logo only, no slogan pile-up.

### Stories (9:16)
- Keep all type in the top 40 percent: wordmark, 2-line headline, 1 to 2 line subhead, pill. Keep the bottom 20 percent clear for the CTA sticker (Blueground's layout exactly).
- Primary text is mostly hidden, so the on-image headline and link headline must carry the message alone.
- One person or one place filling the lower half, shot with room above the head.
- Retargeting stories can use a listing card with price and a "Still thinking about it?" hook (Blueground DPA), but keep owner stories free of urgency.
